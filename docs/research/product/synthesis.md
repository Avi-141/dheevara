## The five decisions this research puts in front of you

### 1. Pick the pilot arc from the Rāmāyaṇa, timed to the film

*Ramayana: Rise of a Legend* (Part 1) opens worldwide on **6 Nov 2026**, two days before Diwali; Part 2 is due at Diwali 2027 (29 Oct). Each release floods search and family conversation with one story. The plan already says "ride the film calendar".

Recommendation: keep the chakravyūha (s1e3) as the pipeline's v0 test, but make the **pilot arc Sundara Kāṇḍa** (Hanumān's leap to Laṅkā, already the next JS experience, Mainaka). Ship free, sourced pieces the week of 6 Nov (the Mainaka experience, two or three Shorts, one audio chapter), positioned as "what Vālmīki's text actually says", and build the paid season toward the spring festivals and Part 2.

### 4. Rewrite the "authority" claim

Mythik has named scholar advisers (Prof. Diwakar Acharya, Oxford; Prof. Parimal Patil, Harvard, per a February 2026 job post) and says its stories are "researched by experts". "Nobody sources" is no longer true. What nobody does is **show** it: a citation on every line, the scanned page one tap away, and a reply from a named scholar when a viewer asks. The plan's comparison table and all marketing should say that.

## What the product must do anyway (compliance)

India's rules on synthetically generated content have been in force since **20 Feb 2026**. Lawyer review is still needed before anything is public. Until then, design to the strictest reading:

1. A prominent visible AI label on every scene, burned into the frames of anything shared outside the app (Shorts, Reels, WhatsApp clips). The app already keeps the label on screen; a 30 Mar 2026 draft would make it continuous for the whole video, and the player has that setting.
2. A **spoken AI disclosure** before AI narration and before the chant, including with the screen off. This is new: add a two-second disclosure line per language to the voice pipeline.
3. Permanent provenance metadata with a unique id that the assembler writes and never strips (C2PA).
4. Takedown within 3 hours of a lawful order: an operational runbook, not a feature.
5. If Dheevara counts as an OTT publisher: an age rating and content descriptor at the start of each chapter, and a grievance officer. Lawyer to confirm.
6. Apple guideline 1.1.5 bars inaccurate quotation of religious texts; showing the source verse is also store protection.

## Channels, in the order the numbers support

1. **WhatsApp family groups**, through the reveal share card (s16/s19).
2. **Bala Vihar and Sunday schools:** Chinmaya 18,000+ children at 49+ North American centres on a 30-week syllabus; BAPS 116 North American mandirs (5,500 children at 2024 camps; Gujarati); HSS 267 US chapters; ISKCON. Offer a **Bala Vihar pack**: chapter, reveal, a short quiz and a people card per week, matched to their syllabus. Kids' heritage apps (Gurukula, Lipi Epics) already use the śloka card; none cite sources.
3. **Temple group viewing**, the *Mahavatar Narsimha* playbook: simultaneous multilingual release, ISKCON group bookings, and word of mouth (week 2 up 64% over week 1).
4. **Distribution partners:** Sri Mandir and temple apps for festival bundles. Every licensing deal carries placement terms (Kuku promoted its devotional app beside an erotic one).

## Lessons from competitors to build in

- Pocket FM's coin complaints ("$1,000+ to finish a story") and Kuku's ₹1–2 trials that set up autopay are the market's top grievances. Our "no coins, no trick trials" belongs on the paywall and in the store listing, with a renewal reminder before every charge.
- The AI Mahabharat's named failures were broken lip-sync, stiff faces and modern props (a bedside table in Hastinapur). The style bible's no-close-up, no-lip-sync rules and the QC never-list address exactly these; ship them before any public clip.
- Pocket FM's Sherpa (19 Sep 2026) explicitly places "moments where listeners are most likely to keep going". That is our retention beat; measure it per shot, as the studio screens do.
- Avoid an Amar Chitra Katha look; ACK is moving into immersive experiences and could be a licensing partner instead.
